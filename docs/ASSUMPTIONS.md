## 2. Judgement calls in the equity bridge and set-up

| Item | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Valuation date | 31 December 2025 (latest full-year balance sheet). FY2026 is forecast year 1. Values are rolled forward to the 6 November 2026 price date at the cost of equity, minus the ₱0.24 dividend paid on 21 May 2026. |
| EBITDA measure | Core EBITDA as reported by Monde (excludes impairments and fair-value changes on the guaranty asset), because those items are not part of recurring operations. |
| Acceptances and trust receipts | Treated as debt: they are short-term bank trade financing that carries interest. |
| Lease liabilities | Treated as debt: under PFRS 16, lease payments are not deducted in EBITDA, so the lease obligation must be deducted in the bridge. |
| Dividends payable | Treated as debt-like: ₱0.16 per share declared in November 2025 and paid in January 2026 was still inside year-end cash. |
| Pension liability | Treated as debt-like (a promise to pay employees), at its full balance-sheet value (not tax-adjusted, a conservative choice). |
| Guaranty asset | Added as a non-operating asset (₱7.71bn, about ₱0.43 per share) because its fair-value changes are excluded from core earnings and therefore from FCFF. Its value can change: it fell by ₱1.09bn in 2025. |
| Current financial assets, FVOCI assets, associates and JVs | Added as non-operating assets: their income is not in core EBITDA. |
| Excluded items | Noncurrent receivables (₱1.43bn) and the derivative liability (₱0.66bn) are excluded: their nature is not clear from the press release. Including both would add about ₱0.04 per share. |
| Minority interest | Book value (₱0.16bn); it is small, so the choice barely matters. |
| Diluted shares | 17,968,611,496 common shares outstanding (PSE EDGE, 4 Nov 2026). No dilutive instruments are disclosed in the sources used; if any exist, value per share is slightly overstated. |
| Comparable companies | Six listed Asian packaged-food companies: two Philippine (URC, Century Pacific), two Indonesian (Indofood CBP, Mayora), one Japanese (Nissin Foods, a noodle peer) and one Thai (Thai President Foods, maker of MAMA noodles).
